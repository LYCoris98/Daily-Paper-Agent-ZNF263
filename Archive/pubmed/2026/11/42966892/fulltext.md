Distant metastasis remains the leading cause of colorectal cancer (CRC) mortality, yet the underlying molecular mechanisms remain incompletely defined. Integrating RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) profiling and clinical validation, we identified NDFIP2 as significantly downregulated in metastatic CRC. Functionally, NDFIP2 loss drove epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), stemness, and metastasis via PI3K/AKT hyperactivation. Mechanistically, NDFIP2 serves as an adaptor recruiting the E3 ligase NEDD4 to induce K48-linked polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of DDX3X. Unrestrained DDX3X resolves R-loops at the GNG13 promoter and recruits the transcription factor CTCF, synergistically enhancing GNG13 transcription to amplify PI3K/AKT signaling. Targeting this vulnerability, we engineered a cell-permeable NDFIP2-derived iRGD-conjugated peptide (ND-iRGD). In vivo administration of this construct suppressed DDX3X/GNG13/PI3K/AKT signaling, thereby attenuating distant metastasis and prolonging murine survival. Collectively, NDFIP2 acts as a critical metastasis suppressor via the DDX3X/R-loop/GNG13 axis, presenting the ND-iRGD peptide as a promising translatable therapeutic strategy for metastatic CRC.
