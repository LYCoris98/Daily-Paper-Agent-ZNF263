Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a common malignancy associated with Wnt/beta-catenin pathway dysregulation. While miRNAs are known to influence cancer, miR-646's role in CRC remains unclear. This study employed qPCR, Western blot, IHC, CCK-8, and Transwell assays to assess miR-646 expression, protein levels, and CRC cell functions. Wnt/beta-catenin activation was analyzed using IF, TOP/FOP reporter assays, and gene interactions were examined through ChIP and dual-luciferase systems. Nude mouse models and clinical samples were used to further evaluate the biological and clinical relevance of miR-646 in CRC. Results showed miR-646 was downregulated in CRC. Its upregulation suppressed CRC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion by inhibiting WNT2B and Wnt/beta-catenin activation. CTCF negatively regulated miR-646 expression, potentially through its binding to the miR-646 promoter. In vivo, miR-646 knockdown promoted CRC xenograft tumor growth, accompanied by increased WNT2B expression and enhanced Wnt/beta-catenin signaling. Additionally, higher miR-646 expression was associated with a better prognosis in patients with CRC. These findings support a role for miR-646 in regulating WNT2B/Wnt/beta-catenin signaling in CRC and demonstrate its negative regulation by CTCF, suggesting the potential of miR-646 as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.
