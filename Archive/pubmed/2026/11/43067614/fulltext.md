The insulin-like growth factor 2 receptor gene (IGF2R) is a maternally-expressed developmental regulator in therian mammals, eutherians and marsupials. In mice the paternal allele of Igf2r is silenced by transcriptional overlap from a ~108 kb antisense Igf2r RNA (Airn) expressed from a differentially methylated region (DMR) in intron 2. In tammar wallabies there is also an antisense transcript (ALID), but it is short, ~650 bp, and it is transcribed from a DMR in intron 12. Here, the structure of the IGF2R DMR and ALID was investigated to evaluate its evolutionary similarity across mammals. A lack of sequence similarity between the intragenic CpG island in eutherians (euCGI-2) and marsupials (maCGI-2) suggested independent acquisition in each lineage. In tammars, maCGI-2 had active histone marks and promoter activity. Transcription from maCGI-2 was complex, with multiple sense and antisense transcripts. Low-level detection of a long-ALID transcript supported a model in which IGF2R imprinting is achieved by transcriptional overlap in marsupials as in eutherians. The maCGI-2 had CTCF sites and was a boundary for 3D interactions with the IGF2R start site and a broader domain containing the maternally-expressed SLC22A3 gene ~250 kb away. These findings indicate that maCGI-2 is a site of complex transcriptional regulation and is a structurally important feature within IGF2R and the broader region.
